# Rental requests

A rental request is how a builder asks to rent your equipment. It is the first step of every rental: request, quote, acceptance, fulfilment and completion.

## What a request contains

A builder sends a request against one of your published listings. It includes:

- **the listing** they want to rent from;
- **a start date** and **an end date**;
- **a message**, if the builder chose to add one, for example about the site or the job.

Builders can send requests only for Active listings. Draft and Inactive listings are not visible to them.

## Request status

A new request has the status `PENDING`. It waits for you to respond. You can:

- **Accept** the request, meaning you are willing to prepare a quote for it; or
- **Decline** it, which ends the request. See "Declining a request".

## Accepting does not commit you

Accepting a request does not create a booking and does not reserve a machine. It means you will prepare a quote.

Nothing is committed until the builder accepts a quote. Until then, neither you nor the builder is bound to the rental.

## What happens next

After you accept a request:

1. Prepare a quote with a `RENTAL` line (the day rate times the rental period), and add `TRANSPORT`, `OPERATOR`, `DEPOSIT` or `DISCOUNT` lines where they apply. GST at 18% is added.
2. Send the quote. Once sent, it is locked and cannot be edited.
3. If the builder accepts, a booking is created immediately with the status `CONFIRMED`.

If your listing has a rate card, the system may draft the quote for you. An automatic draft starts as `PENDING_APPROVAL` and is not shown to the builder until you review and send it.

## Before accepting

Check that:

- at least one machine on the listing is free for the requested dates, with no block or booking covering them;
- you can deliver to the builder's site;
- the builder's message does not ask for anything you cannot provide.

## Responding promptly

Builders often send requests when they are planning a job. A quick answer, whether accepting or declining, helps them plan.
